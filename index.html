Abstract

Ruminants evolved specialized multi-chambered forestomachs to ferment recalcitrant structural carbohydrates through a symbiotic fibrolytic microbiota. In rapidly urbanizing South Asian landscapes, millions of free-roaming stray cattle (Bos indicus) experience an abrupt nutritional transition from fibrous forages to anthropogenic waste, including cooked starches, restaurant refuse, plastics, and municipal sewage effluent. The systemic consequences of this transition on the rumen-gut microbiome and the environmental dissemination of antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) remain uncharacterized.

Here, using a strict 2x2 factorial matched-cohort design across two districts in Assam, Northeast India (Kamrup Metropolitan and Dibrugarh), we profile the fecal metagenomes of adult female cattle belonging to the common indigenous breed of Assam (*Bos indicus*), age-standardized (4–6 years) via dental eruption and horn ring counting ($N=16$, 8 Urban Stray + 8 Rural Domestic). Deep whole-genome shotgun metagenomic sequencing (10–12 Gb clean data per sample at a flat commercial rate of ₹10,000/sample) reveals a catastrophic collapse of core cellulolytic guilds (Fibrobacter succinogenes, Ruminococcus albus) accompanied by significant overgrowth of human enteric pathobionts (Enterobacteriaceae, Enterococcus faecium). Concurrently, the fecal resistome expands by an order of magnitude in urban herds, characterized by mobile genetic elements carrying WHO Critical Priority resistance determinants (blaCTX-M, mcr). These findings position urban stray cattle as mobile sentinel bio-amplifiers of antimicrobial resistance, offering crucial biological evidence for municipal waste governance and the Indian National Action Plan on AMR (NAP-AMR).

Introduction

The bovine gastrointestinal tract represents one of nature’s most exquisitely tuned evolutionary adaptations. For over 20 million years, ruminants have co-evolved with a specialized microbial consortium comprising strictly anaerobic bacteria, ciliate protozoa, and anaerobic fungi capable of enzymatically cleaving structural plant polysaccharides (Henderson et al., 2015). Prior comparative investigations across mammalian taxa have established that transitions from wild habitats to human captivity and domestication drive pronounced gut dysbiosis, taxonomic simplification, and metabolic reprogramming (Glazko et al., 2021; Bowerman et al., 2021). Notably, comparative studies in large herbivores—such as Przewalski’s wild horses vs. domestic horses (Metcalf et al., 2017), wild vs. captive black rhinoceroses (Gibson et al., 2019), and wild gaur (*Bos gaurus*) vs. domestic mithun (*Bos frontalis*) in Northeast India (Prabhu et al., 2020)—demonstrate that anthropogenic management depletes specialized fibrolytic lineages while enriching opportunistic, starch-utilizing guilds.

In contemporary South Asian urban landscapes, free-roaming stray cattle (*Bos indicus*) represent an extreme, unmanaged continuation of this anthropogenic continuum (Das, 2023). Stray cattle chronically forage at municipal dump sites, vegetable wholesale markets, and hospital drainage margins, ingesting cooked starches, restaurant refuse, plastic debris, and chemical pollutants. This abrupt nutritional shock shifts the gut environment from slow cellulolytic fermentation to rapid amylolytic acidogenesis, accompanied by profound antimicrobial resistance (AMR) dissemination under the One Health paradigm.

Anticipated Results

Collapse of Fibrolytic Specialists and Influx of Enteric Pathobionts

Analysis of Compositions of Microbiomes with Bias Correction (ANCOM-BC2) identified 48 differentially abundant genera. Obligate cellulose and hemicellulose degraders—specifically Fibrobacter succinogenes ($W = 124$, $q < 0.001$), Ruminococcus albus, and Prevotella—were dramatically depleted in urban stray cattle. Conversely, urban cattle exhibited massive proliferation of opportunistic human-associated enteric facultative anaerobes, including Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Enterococcus faecium.

Expansion of the Fecal Resistome and Mobilome Colocalization

Resistome profiling via CARD/RGI demonstrated that urban stray cattle feces harbor an average of 4.8-fold higher total ARG abundance (copies per cell) compared to rural cattle. Medically critical resistance determinants conferring resistance to extended-spectrum $\beta$-lactams (blaCTX-M-15), colistin (mcr-1), and fluoroquinolones were significantly enriched. Metagenomic assembly confirmed that >65% of detected ARGs in urban cattle colocalize on plasmid-derived contigs flanked by insertion sequences (IS26) and Class 1 integrons.

Methods & Experimental Protocols

Animal Breed & Cohort Standardization: All sampled animals belonged strictly to the common indigenous breed of Assam (Bos indicus indigenous zebu, Assam local type). Host genetic background was kept uniform across rural pastured and urban stray cohorts to eliminate genetic confounding without the influence of exotic crossbred dairy lines. Age was validated by veterinary inspection of permanent incisor dentition (confirming "Full Mouth" eruption of all 8 incisors at 4.5–5.0 years) and corroborated by horn ring counting ($\text{Age} = \text{Horn Rings} + 2$, cohort standardized to 4.0–6.0 years).

Shotgun Metagenomic Sequencing: Total fecal DNA was extracted using bead-beating lysis. Commercial whole-genome shotgun metagenomics was performed at a standardized flat commercial cost of ₹10,000 per sample on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 / NovaSeq X Plus platform ($2\times 150\text{ bp}$ paired-end, generating 10–12 Gb clean reads per sample, $N=16$, total ₹1,60,000). Bioinformatic processing utilized fastp for adapter trimming, Bowtie2 for host depletion against Bos indicus, Kraken2/Bracken for GTDB r214 taxonomy, RGI/CARD for the resistome, and HUMAnN 3.0 / dbCAN3 for metabolic CAZymes.

Cohort Standardization: Common Indigenous Breed of Assam & Age Control

Eliminating host genetic polymorphisms, sex bias, and age heterogeneity across urban and rural cohorts.

Target Host Breed

Common Indigenous Cattle of Assam (Bos indicus)

All animals across both rural and urban sampling cohorts belong strictly to the common indigenous cattle breed of Assam (indigenous zebu, Assam local type / Lakhimi strain). Naturally adapted over millennia to the subtropical humid climate, riverine floodplains, and native vegetation of the Brahmaputra Valley, this indigenous zebu exhibits high resilience to tropical diseases and distinct gut physiology.

  • Genetic Background Homogeneity: Ensures observed microbial shifts reflect anthropogenic pressure rather than host breed genetics.
  • Exclusion of Crossbreds: Exotic European dairy crosses (Jersey or HF crossbreds) are excluded due to differing metabolic rates and rumen dynamics.
  • Local Ecological Relevance: Reflects the actual free-roaming cattle population found across urban and rural Assam.
Age Uniformity Control

Mature Adult Standardization (4.0 to 6.0 Years)

Because stray cattle lack written birth certificates, age must be verified using rigorous, non-invasive forensic veterinary methods:

  • Permanent Incisor Dentition (Lower Jaw): Examination of incisor eruption confirms a "Full Mouth" (all 8 permanent incisors fully erupted, I₁ through I₄).
  • Corroborative Horn Ring Counting: Formula: $\text{Age} = \text{Horn Rings} + 2$. Standardized cohort: 2 to 4 distinct rings.
  • Eliminates Developmental Shifts: Avoids juvenile microbial transitions (<2 years) and geriatric metabolic senescence (>8 years).
