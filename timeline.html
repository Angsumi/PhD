Research phases
in detail.

Each phase builds on the previous one. Overlapping activities ensure continuous progress without bottlenecks.

Phase 1

Literature review & study design

Months 1 – 6

Build the theoretical foundation. Survey existing research on cattle gut microbiomes, One Health frameworks, AMR in livestock, and plant-diet-microbiome interactions. Define hypotheses and finalise the sampling strategy.

Systematic review

Catalogue existing studies on cattle metagenomics, humanization signatures, and AMR transfer pathways.

Study design

Finalise sample size (200 cattle), site selection across 3 climatic regions, and metadata collection protocols.

Ethics & permissions

Obtain IAEC approval, MoU with partner farms, and permissions for stray cattle sampling in urban areas.

Pilot sampling

Test collection, storage, and DNA extraction protocols with a small batch to validate the workflow.

Phase 2

Field sampling & metadata collection

Months 5 – 12

Collect fecal samples from 100 domestic and 100 stray cattle across urban and rural settings. Record management practices, antibiotic history, human contact level, GPS location, and health status for each animal. Collect blood/PBMC from a matched subset of 30–40 animals.

Domestic cattle sampling

100 animals from managed farms — fresh fecal samples, blood draws for the RNA-seq subset, and detailed husbandry records.

Stray cattle sampling

100 free-roaming animals from urban/peri-urban areas — fecal collection, observational health scoring, and environmental context notes.

Metadata questionnaires

Standardised forms for diet, antibiotic exposure, contact frequency, housing, and local environmental conditions.

Sample processing & storage

Aliquot, preserve in DNA/RNA stabilisers, and store at −80 °C within 4 hours of collection.

Phase 3

DNA/RNA extraction & sequencing

Months 10 – 18

Extract high-quality nucleic acids from all samples. Perform whole-genome shotgun metagenomics on all 200 fecal samples. For the matched subset, add microbial metatranscriptomics and host blood/PBMC RNA-seq. Run plant DNA metabarcoding using chloroplast markers (trnL, rbcL, matK).

Fecal DNA extraction

High-quality DNA from 200 samples for shotgun metagenomics and plant metabarcoding.

Shotgun metagenomics

Paired-end whole-genome sequencing for broad community and functional coverage.

Metatranscriptomics

Microbial RNA-seq from ~30–40 matched samples to capture active gene expression.

Host RNA-seq & plant metabarcoding

Blood/PBMC RNA-seq (15–20 per group) and fecal plant DNA profiling via chloroplast markers.

Phase 4

Parasitology & resistome screening

Months 12 – 18

Examine fecal samples by microscopy and molecular confirmation for macroparasite diversity and zoonotic risk. Screen metagenomic reads against CARD, ResFinder, and MEGARes databases to map antimicrobial resistance gene profiles and assess mobility elements.

Fecal microscopy

Identify helminth eggs, protozoan cysts, and other macroparasites using standard flotation and sedimentation techniques.

Molecular parasite confirmation

PCR-based confirmation of key zoonotic parasites detected microscopically.

Resistome profiling

Screen shotgun reads against CARD, ResFinder, and MEGARes for resistance gene abundance, diversity, and co-occurrence.

Mobility element analysis

Identify integrons, plasmid-associated ARGs, and insertion sequences linked to horizontal gene transfer.

Phase 5

Bioinformatics & data analysis

Months 16 – 28

Process all sequencing data through quality filtering, host DNA removal, and taxonomic/functional annotation. Compare alpha and beta diversity, differential abundance, functional pathway enrichment, and dietary plant profiles between domestic and stray groups.

Quality control & preprocessing

Adapter trimming, quality filtering, bovine DNA decontamination, and read-level QC across all datasets.

Taxonomic & functional profiling

Classify bacteria, archaea, viruses, and eukaryotes; annotate KEGG pathways, CAZymes, and virulence factors.

Differential analysis

Compare community composition, diversity metrics, and functional pathways between domestic vs. stray and urban vs. rural groups.

Host transcriptome analysis

Differential gene expression of immune, inflammatory, metabolic, and epithelial-barrier modules from blood/PBMC RNA-seq.

Phase 6

Multi-omics integration & modelling

Months 24 – 32

Connect all six datasets into a unified model: plant/diet exposure → gut microbiome composition → microbial gene activity → host gene expression → AMR and zoonotic risk. Use multivariate statistics and machine learning to identify the "humanization signature".

Correlation networks

Build co-occurrence networks linking plant diet profiles, microbial taxa, active pathways, and host gene modules.

Machine learning models

Train classifiers to predict domestic vs. stray origin from microbiome features; identify the strongest predictive signatures.

Causal pathway modelling

Use structural equation modelling or mediation analysis to test the diet → microbiome → host response chain.

One Health risk scoring

Combine resistome, parasite, and zoonotic microbe data into a composite risk framework for public health interpretation.

Phase 7

Thesis writing & defence

Months 28 – 36

Draft the thesis, prepare manuscripts for peer-reviewed publication, present findings at conferences, and submit for defence. Translate key findings into actionable recommendations for livestock management and One Health policy.

Manuscript preparation

Prepare 3–4 manuscripts covering metagenomics, multi-omics integration, resistome/parasitology, and the One Health framework.

Thesis compilation

Integrate all chapters — introduction, methods, results, discussion — into a unified thesis document.

Conference presentations

Present findings at national/international conferences on microbiome science, One Health, or veterinary public health.

Defence & policy brief

Defend the thesis and prepare a policy brief summarising actionable findings for livestock and public health stakeholders.
